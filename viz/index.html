Football has been played in the Amazon at 32°C and in air-conditioned deserts, so it is tempting to assume heat changes the match. Tested against the real temperature of every match, it does not. What moves scoring is the era it was played in.

Read this honestly: point colour shows goals scored, not temperature. Hover any match to pull its real recorded temperature. Across 1,014 matches with weather data, temperature has no significant effect on goals, either on its own (p = 0.77) or after holding era, host, humidity and latitude constant (p = 0.19). What does move scoring is era: goals fell from 5.4 per match in 1954 to 2.2 in 1990 as tactics tightened. Goals is also a coarse measure, so heat may still affect distance covered or second-half fade, which would need event-level data to see.
